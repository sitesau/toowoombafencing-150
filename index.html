When a Repair Might Be Better Than Replacement

The source material treats fence repairs as a distinct consideration, not merely a less expensive alternative to full replacement. Issues such as leaning posts, broken rails, or storm-damaged sections can often be repaired effectively. However, a complete replacement might be more sensible if the entire fence run is no longer structurally sound. To prepare for this discussion, homeowners can photograph the affected fence line from both sides where access permits, note whether the damage is isolated or widespread, and state if the fence incorporates a gate. If only a single bay is damaged but adjacent posts are also moving, the scope of work may need to expand. If the entire fence has failed, requesting both repair and replacement options can facilitate a clearer comparison of quotes.

Quote Details Worth Asking For

A productive fencing discussion translates site conditions into clearly itemised inclusions. Enquire about what is covered for removal, disposal, posts, concrete footings, gate hardware, automation systems, returns, and any changes in direction along the fence line. If there are shared costs with a neighbour, it is important to separate the discussion about a sufficient dividing fence from any upgrades, such as premium finishes or increased height. The source material also advises verifying the QBCC licensing for any work that requires it, ensuring the contractor holds the correct scope of licence for the job. This is a practical verification step before relying on a contractor for regulated work. For dividing fences in Queensland, the process is governed by the Neighbourhood Disputes (Dividing Fences and Trees) Act 2011, with QCAT serving as the forum for disputes that cannot be resolved amicably.

  1. Describe the Fence Run. Provide the length of the fence line, its location on the property, any limitations on access for workers and machinery, and whether an existing fence needs to be removed.
  2. Name the Fence's Purpose. Clearly state the primary function of the fence, such as enhancing privacy, ensuring pool safety, improving street presentation, containing livestock on a rural property, providing security, or addressing storm damage.
  3. Flag Complications Early. Mention any site-specific challenges like significant ground slope, the presence of retaining walls, corner block sight line requirements, hard ground conditions, the need for gates, or involvement of neighbours before the quote is prepared.
  4. Check the Written Quote. Compare all inclusions and exclusions, specified materials, details for gates and automation, and the contractor's QBCC licence scope before considering quotes equivalent based on price alone.

Common questions

What is the first step when enquiring about a new fence in Toowoomba? Start by defining the boundary line, assessing the site conditions (ground, access), and determining the intended use of the fence. This provides a solid foundation for your initial enquiry and subsequent quotes.

How are dividing fences handled in Queensland? Queensland's Neighbourhood Disputes (Dividing Fences and Trees) Act 2011 outlines a process for adjoining owners. Generally, there is a one-month period to reach an agreement. If unresolved, the matter may proceed to QCAT.

What should I check regarding a contractor's licence? For any fencing work that requires licensing, it is essential to verify that the contractor holds a current QBCC licence with the appropriate scope of work for the specific job you need done.
